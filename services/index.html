Market Opportunity Assessment

Miru Partners assesses each candidate market at the level of the specific segment your product would compete in, pairing quantitative modelling with intelligence gathered on the ground. The work analyses which market offers the strongest return on the investment entry requires, and over what timeframe.

The figures that decide a market seldom appear in published data. Distributor margins, customary payment terms and the time a buyer takes to approve a new supplier are learned through conversation, and Miru Partners gathers them directly from people active in each market. That evidence often reorders a shortlist. A market that looks modest on paper can prove far more accessible once sales cycles and margins are factored in.

What the Assessment Covers

  • Demand analysis by customer type and region
  • Competitor mapping, including informal and regional players that seldom appear in industry reports
  • Margin modelling after local distribution costs, duties and payment terms
  • Certification and regulatory hurdles specific to your product category
  • Interviews with prospective buyers and channel partners

What Your Board Receives

A ranked shortlist of markets, with the assumptions behind each recommendation set out so the board can test the reasoning before approving the budget. Each market comes with an estimated entry cost and a realistic timeline to first revenue, giving your finance and commercial teams a shared basis for planning. Where a market falls short, the assessment names the conditions that would change the verdict, so the option can be revisited when circumstances shift.

A European Plan With Too Many Destinations

The client, a Boston provider of workflow software for logistics operators, had closed a Series B round, and its board wanted a European presence within the year. The leadership team had a list of eleven possible markets, drawn from inbound leads and investor suggestions, and almost every executive had a favourite. Germany led the internal vote on size alone.

Testing Each Market Against the Client's Own Economics

Miru Partners began by modelling what a customer was worth in each market once local implementation costs and the typical length of an enterprise sales cycle were taken into account. Interviews followed with logistics operators and with the integrators who would resell and implement the software.

Two findings changed the shortlist. German operators expected German-language interfaces and on-site support, which would have meant hiring a local team well before any revenue arrived. Dutch operators were happy to buy in English, and many of them run pan-European networks from Rotterdam and Schiphol. They saw the software as a way to coordinate operations across several countries at once.

Why the Netherlands Came First

The final recommendation ranked the Netherlands first, with Belgium as a natural second phase. Germany was deferred until a Dutch customer base could provide references. Each market in the ranking came with an estimated entry cost and a projected timeline to first revenue, which the client's CFO used to set the European budget.

Five Months to a Live Customer

After board approval, Miru Partners introduced the client to two Dutch logistics integrators already serving the target segment, and both signed channel agreements. The first enterprise customer went live five months after the recommendation was accepted. It was a contract logistics provider operating across the Benelux. Since then, the client has opened its first Belgian accounts through the same partners.

A Shared Language and a Default Destination

The client, a Montreal manufacturer of point-of-care diagnostic devices, had recently secured CE marking for its flagship product. With a French-speaking leadership team and sales materials already in French, France felt like the natural first market. The sales team had started approaching French hospitals before any strategy was agreed, and the responses were courteous but noncommittal.

Mapping How Hospitals Actually Buy

Miru Partners’ assessment focused on how purchasing decisions are made in each market. In France, much of the hospital purchasing ran through large purchasing groups on multi-year framework contracts. Established suppliers held those contracts, and the next renewal window was some way off.

Interviews with procurement officers and clinicians in Sweden and Denmark painted a different picture. Regional authorities there publish their tenders openly and run them more often, which gives a new supplier regular chances to compete. Clinicians were also actively looking for faster diagnostics to ease waiting times in emergency departments. Margin modelling confirmed that Nordic price points would protect the client's margins after distributor fees. Winning a French framework contract, by contrast, would have demanded volume discounts the client could not sustain.

Turning a Smaller Market into a Stronger Launch

Miru Partners recommended Sweden as the lead market, followed by Denmark, with France moved to a second phase timed around the next framework renewals. The reasoning was that a few Nordic regional contracts would provide the clinical evidence and user references French purchasing groups look for when assessing a new supplier. Miru Partners also found a Swedish distributor experienced in public tenders and prepared the documentation the client would need to bid.

A Tender Won and France Within Reach

Ten months after the assessment, the client won its first regional tender in Sweden, covering emergency departments across several hospitals. Denmark followed with a pilot at a university hospital. When the client went back to French hospitals with Nordic outcome data in hand, two university hospitals agreed to formal product evaluations ahead of the next tender round.

Partner and Distributor Introductions

Choosing a distributor is one of the most consequential commercial decisions in any market entry, because exclusivity and territory terms can bind a company for years. Miru Partners identifies candidates through first-hand knowledge of who carries which brands and how well they perform. Miru Partners then manages the process from the first approach to a signed agreement.

Many partnerships fail on fit long before they fail on performance. A distributor with impressive reach may already carry a competing line or may lack the technical sales staff your product depends on. Miru Partners screens for these issues before any approach is made, so the partners you meet are the ones worth negotiating with. Once talks begin, Miru Partners’ knowledge of what comparable brands have agreed in that market gives you a realistic benchmark for margins and minimum volumes.

How Each Introduction is Prepared

  • A partner profile defining the reach, sector focus and capacity your product needs
  • Checks on each candidate's financial standing and any conflicts within their current portfolio
  • A briefing that frames your offer in the partner's own commercial terms
  • Miru Partners’ involvement in early meetings and through negotiation
  • Guidance on exclusivity, territory and performance clauses

The Result

A partner whose incentives match yours, signed on terms that keep your options open if performance falls short. Agreements include measurable targets and review dates, so any underperformance can be handled through the contract itself. Miru Partners stays available through the first months of the partnership, helping both sides settle into a working rhythm and resolve the early friction that new distribution relationships tend to produce.

Entry Strategy and Due Diligence

An entry strategy sets the legal and financial framework of an expansion, from the entity a company trades through to the order in which it commits capital. Miru Partners tests each option against your growth plans and appetite for risk. Before any agreement is signed, Miru Partners also examines the counterparties you would rely on.

The right structure on day one is rarely the right structure in year three. Many expansions begin through an agent or distributor and move to a subsidiary once volumes justify the overhead. Planning that transition from the start avoids renegotiating contracts or unwinding arrangements later. Miru Partners maps those points in advance, tying each change of entry model to the commercial milestone that would justify it.

What the Strategy Includes

  • A comparison of entry models, from agent arrangements to a branch, subsidiary or joint venture
  • A licensing and regulatory review for your sector
  • Due diligence on prospective partners, acquisition targets or key suppliers
  • A resourcing plan for first hires, budgets and reporting lines
  • Coordination with legal and tax specialists from Miru Partners’ affiliate firms

The Result

A plan your board can approve and your team can carry out, with review points marked where early results should inform the next decision. Each review point is linked to a measurable trigger, such as sales volume or headcount, so any deeper investment follows evidence. Miru Partners briefs your legal and tax specialists in advance, which keeps their input focused and their fees in proportion to the work.

A German Engineering Firm's Staged Move from Sales Agent to Dutch BV

Miru Partners designed a two-stage entry that let a German pump manufacturer test the Dutch market through a commercial agent before committing to a subsidiary. Because the changeover was planned from the start, the move to a Dutch BV took place without losing a single customer or getting into a dispute with the agent.

Read the full case study
Demand Without a Local Footprint

The client, a German manufacturer of pumps for municipal water treatment, was winning Dutch enquiries without any local presence. Its board was split. One side wanted to open a subsidiary straight away, while the other preferred to appoint an agent and keep fixed costs low.

The Clause Most Agent Agreements Overlook

Miru Partners recommended starting with an agent, but with a structure few agent agreements include. Dutch law entitles a commercial agent to a goodwill indemnity when the agreement ends, which can make moving later to a subsidiary costly and contentious. Miru Partners drafted the arrangement with the client's legal advisers so that the transition to a BV, the handover of customer relationships and the agent's compensation were agreed in advance. Due diligence on the shortlisted agent also confirmed that the agent represented no competing product lines among Dutch water boards.

Triggers in Place of Guesswork

The strategy set two measurable triggers for setting up the BV. The first was annual Dutch revenue passing an agreed threshold. The second was the point at which service contracts required permanent local technicians. Both were reviewed each quarter against the agent's pipeline.

A Seamless Conversion

Both triggers were met within eighteen months, and the client incorporated its Dutch BV in Rotterdam. The agent joined the new entity as its commercial director, bringing every existing customer relationship along. Two water board framework contracts were signed in the BV's first year.

Miru Partners redesigned a UK manufacturer's plans for France, replacing an expensive Paris subsidiary with a phased model that let revenue fund each step. Read the full case study
A Subsidiary Planned Before the First Sale

The client, a UK manufacturer of commercial kitchen ventilation systems, had a board-approved plan to open a French subsidiary with a Paris office and a sales team of four. The business case assumed revenue that the company had not yet tested in France.

Following the Customers South

Miru Partners’ analysis showed that the client's natural customers were hotel groups and restaurant chains, with much of their refurbishment activity concentrated around Lyon and along the Mediterranean coast. Miru Partners proposed a first phase built around a network of French commercial agents supported by an established installation partner in Lyon. Opening a subsidiary was deferred until the installed base needed local after-sales staff.

The Insurance Question that Changed the Shortlist

French clients expect the installers of building systems to hold garantie décennale, the ten-year liability insurance that covers defects in construction work. Due diligence found that the installation partner the client preferred lacked adequate cover for ventilation work. Had installations gone ahead, the client's own reputation would have been exposed. Miru Partners switched to a second partner with full cover before any contract was signed.

Revenue First, Structure Second

The client met its first-year French sales target through the agent network, at a fraction of the fixed costs in the original subsidiary plan. With a growing installed base around Lyon, the board has now approved a French subsidiary for its second year. The subsidiary will be funded from French revenue and located where the customers are.

Dutch Market Set-Up

For companies setting up in the Netherlands, Miru Partners handles the administration in the order Dutch authorities and banks expect. Sequence matters here: banks check registrations before opening accounts, and a VAT number follows registration with the KvK. Clients are guided through every step in English, and Miru Partners deals with notaries and providers wherever Dutch is required.

Foreign founders often lose weeks to steps that look simple from the outside. Typical examples are opening a bank account without a local track record or recording ownership in a way that satisfies Dutch requirements and the parent company's structure at the same time. Miru Partners anticipates these points before anything is submitted, preparing documentation that satisfies the notary and the bank first time. The set-up is also matched to your commercial plan, so the entity and payment arrangements suit how the business intends to sell.

What Miru Partners Arranges

  • Advice on the right legal form, plus coordination with a civil-law notary where a BV is required
  • Registration with the KvK, including the UBO register
  • VAT registration and a calendar of upcoming filings
  • A business bank account and a payment provider
  • A professional website with domain email

The Result

A properly registered Dutch business, able to trade, with every filing deadline mapped in advance. You receive a single overview of registrations and recurring obligations, ready to hand to an in-house finance team or an external accountant. If the business later grows its Dutch presence, the groundwork for hiring staff or adding entities is already in place.

An Australian Homeware Brand Trading from Amsterdam Within Six Weeks of Arrival

Miru Partners prepared every registration in advance for an Australian homeware brand, so its Dutch BV, bank account and import arrangements were all in place within six weeks of the founders landing in Amsterdam. The set-up also anticipated how the brand would sell across Europe, saving it from a costly restructure a few months later.

Read the full case study
A Launch Date Already Announced

The client, a Melbourne homeware brand known for ceramics and linen, had announced a European webshop launch to its customers before choosing a legal structure. Its founders planned to move to Amsterdam and expected the paperwork to take a few days. Container stock was already booked to arrive in Rotterdam.

Working Back from the Shipment

Miru Partners planned the set-up backwards from the date the first container would clear customs. The founders' identity and ownership documents were prepared before they left Australia, which meant the civil-law notary could incorporate the BV in their first week. The UBO register entry was completed at the same time as the KvK registration. Business bank accounts for foreign-owned companies often stall at the identity and compliance stage, so Miru Partners briefed the bank in advance with a full ownership chart and the brand's trading history.

Import Arrangements Most Newcomers Miss

Two details protected the client's cash flow. Miru Partners arranged an EORI number so the stock could clear customs under the brand's own name. Miru Partners also applied for an Article 23 licence, the Dutch arrangement that lets importers account for import VAT in their VAT return instead of paying it at the border. Without it, the client would have paid VAT up front on every container. Payments were set up with iDEAL, the method most Dutch shoppers expect at checkout.

Selling to Europe from the First Order

Customers across the EU would be ordering from the webshop, so Miru Partners registered the client for the EU One-Stop Shop scheme. This lets the business report VAT on cross-border consumer sales through a single Dutch return. The webshop opened on its announced date, six weeks after the founders arrived. Orders from Germany and Belgium made up a third of first-month sales.

A Contract Waiting on a Company

The client, a Bengaluru provider of software development and maintenance for the logistics sector, had won a contract with a Rotterdam freight operator. The contract had one condition: the client had to deliver through a Dutch entity with engineers on site. Work was due to start in four months.

Two Timelines Running in Parallel

Miru Partners recognised that the company set-up and the engineers' relocation depended on each other. To hire engineers under the Netherlands' highly skilled migrant scheme, the client first needed recognition as a sponsor from the IND, the Dutch immigration service. The IND would only consider that application once the BV was registered and banking was in place. Miru Partners ran the two processes side by side, bringing in an immigration specialist from its affiliate firms as soon as the notary had a date for incorporation.

Registrations that Serve an Employer

Because the BV would employ staff from its first month, Miru Partners arranged payroll tax registration alongside VAT registration. Employment contracts and salaries were also aligned with the income thresholds that apply to highly skilled migrants. Miru Partners set up invoicing for the freight operator in line with Dutch VAT requirements, and matched the payment terms to the client's cash flow over the first quarter.

Delivered Inside the Window

The client's first three engineers arrived in Rotterdam three weeks before the contract start date, and the first invoice went out at the end of the first month. The BV has since taken on its first locally hired project manager. The client is now preparing a second contract with another logistics operator at the port.
